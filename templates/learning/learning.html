LEVEL 00 · BEGINNER

Stock Market Foundations

How money, companies, and the stock market connect.

★ Start here ⌄
01 💰 Money, Savings & Investment ›

Before understanding stocks, you must understand how money works. People earn money through jobs or business. This money is either spent, saved, or invested.

Spending — using money for daily needs
Saving — keeping money in the bank
Investing — using money to create more money in the future

Savings grow slowly. Investments have the potential to grow faster but come with risk.

Example: If Ramesh from Kathmandu keeps NPR 10,000 in a bank, it grows slowly. But if he invests it in a company, its value may increase based on company performance.
02 🏢 What is a Company? ›

A company is an organization that produces goods or services to earn profit. For example:

A bank like NIC Asia Bank provides financial services
A hydropower company produces electricity
A cement company produces construction materials

Companies need money to operate and grow. This money comes from owners or investors.

03 👥 Ownership in Simple Terms ›

Ownership means having a share of something. If you own a shop completely, you get all the profit. If 10 people own it together, profit is shared among them. Companies also work the same way.

Instead of one owner, many people can own small parts of a company. These small ownership parts are called shares.

04 📦 What is a Share? ›

A share represents a small unit of ownership in a company. If a company is divided into 10,000 shares and you own 100, you own a small portion of that company. This means:

You indirectly own part of its assets
You share its profits and losses
Example: If Sita buys shares of a bank, she becomes a partial owner of that bank, even though she does not manage it.
05 📈 Why Companies Give Shares ›

Companies do not always have enough money to grow. Instead of borrowing everything from banks, they can sell shares to the public. This helps them:

Raise money for expansion
Reduce loan dependency
Grow faster
Example: A hydropower company in Nepal may need NPR 100 crore for a new project. Instead of taking a big loan, it allows the public to invest by buying shares.
06 🌐 What is a Stock Market? ›

A stock market is a system where people buy and sell ownership (shares) of companies. It is not a physical place for most people today — it works digitally.

Think of it as a marketplace where company ownership is traded. People who believe a company will grow buy its shares. People who want to exit sell their shares.

Stock markets exist to connect two groups:

Companies — need money to grow
Investors — want to grow their money

This system helps the overall economy grow.

07 📊 Why Stock Prices Exist ›

Share prices are not fixed. They change based on:

Demand — how many people want to buy
Supply — how many people want to sell
Future expectations of the company
Example: If people believe a hydropower company will earn more profit next year, more people will try to buy its shares, increasing its price.
08 📉 Profit, Loss & Risk ›

Investing in companies is not guaranteed profit.

If a company performs well → share price increases → investors gain profit
If a company performs poorly → share price may fall → investors may lose money
Example: If a cement company's sales drop due to low construction demand, its share price may also decrease.
09 🇳🇵 Nepali Context ›

In Nepal, many sectors depend on the stock market:

Hydropower companies build national electricity projects
Banks support financial growth
Insurance companies provide risk protection

When Nepali people invest in companies, they indirectly participate in national development.

LEVEL 01 · BEGINNER

Getting Started with NEPSE

PAN, DEMAT, MeroShare, broker account and your first trade.

★ Start here ⌄
01 🪪 PAN Card ›

A PAN (Permanent Account Number) is required for opening a DEMAT and broker account in Nepal. It acts as your financial identity for tax and investment purposes.

You can obtain it through the Inland Revenue Department or your bank.

02 🏦 Bank Account Linking ›

An active bank account is necessary for stock market transactions. It is used for IPO fund blocking, dividend payments, refunds, and settlement payments.

Make sure your account details match your other investment accounts.

03 💾 DEMAT Account ›

A DEMAT account stores your shares electronically. Instead of physical certificates, all shares in Nepal are now held digitally.

This account is mandatory for buying, selling, and receiving shares.

04 🔢 BOID Number ›

A BOID is a 16-digit identification number linked to your DEMAT account. It is required when applying for IPOs and receiving shares.

Always double-check your BOID while submitting applications.

05 📱 MeroShare ›

MeroShare is Nepal's online investor portal. It allows users to apply for IPOs, view portfolios, approve EDIS, and manage share-related activities.

It is one of the most frequently used platforms for NEPSE investors.

06 🔑 CRN Number ›

A CRN (C-ASBA Registration Number) is issued by your bank after registering for IPO applications. It links your bank account to the IPO system and allows funds to be blocked during applications.

07 👤 Broker Account ›

A broker account allows you to trade shares in the secondary market. Since investors cannot trade directly on NEPSE, all buying and selling must happen through licensed brokers.

08 💻 TMS Login ›

TMS (Trade Management System) is the online trading portal provided by brokers. It is used to place buy and sell orders, track trades, and monitor market activity in real time.

09 📨 Applying for IPO ›

IPO applications are submitted through MeroShare using your CRN and BOID. The application amount is blocked in your bank account until allotment results are announced.

10 💸 Buying Shares ›

To buy shares, log into TMS, select a company, enter the quantity and price, and place your order. If a seller matches your price, the trade is executed.

11 🔄 Selling Shares ›

Selling shares is done through TMS by placing a sell order. Once matched, the shares are transferred to the buyer and payment is credited after settlement.

12 ✅ EDIS Process ›

After selling shares, you must approve the transaction through EDIS in MeroShare. Without this approval, the share transfer may fail or be delayed.

13 📅 Settlement Cycle ›

NEPSE generally follows a T+2 settlement system. This means shares and payment are usually transferred within two working days after the trade.

14 📊 Portfolio Tracking ›

Portfolio tracking helps you monitor your investments, profits, losses, and diversification. Regular review helps you make smarter investment decisions.

LEVEL 02 · BEGINNER

NEPSE Core Concepts

Types of shares, market data, trading mechanics, and market structure.

★ Start here ⌄
01 📦 Types of Shares & Company Offerings ›

Before understanding IPO, FPO, or bonus shares, you must first understand what types of shares exist in the market. Companies issue shares in different ways depending on their needs and stage of growth.

Ordinary Shares (Public Shares) — most common shares in NEPSE; investors become part-owners and can trade freely in the market
Promoter Shares — held by founders or major stakeholders; usually have restrictions on selling (lock-in period)
IPO (Initial Public Offering) — when a company first sells its shares to the public; the entry point of a company into the stock market
FPO (Further Public Offering) — when a company that is already listed issues additional shares to raise more capital
Bonus Shares — free additional shares given to existing shareholders from company profits
Right Shares — offered only to existing shareholders at a discounted price, allowing them to increase ownership before new investors enter
Example: If Hari owns 100 shares and the company announces a 10% bonus, he receives 10 extra shares without paying anything. A hydropower company launching an IPO allows people like Sita or Ramesh to apply through MeroShare to become early investors.
02 📊 Market Price & Trading Data ›

Once shares are in the market, their prices constantly change. Price is not fixed — it constantly changes based on demand, supply, and investor behavior.

Open Price — the first price at which a share trades when the market opens
LTP (Last Traded Price) — the most recent price at which a share was bought or sold; keeps changing throughout the trading day
CMP (Current Market Price) — real-time market price based on ongoing trades; LTP and CMP are often very close
High & Low Price — highest and lowest price during the day; shows volatility of a stock
Volume — how many shares were traded in a day; high volume means strong interest in that stock
Turnover — total money traded in a stock or market during a day
Example: If NIC Asia Bank opens at NPR 700, that is its opening price. If many investors trade large quantities of Shivam Cement shares, turnover increases.
03 ⚖️ Market Mechanism — How Trading Actually Happens ›

The market is basically a negotiation system between buyers and sellers.

Bid Price — the highest price a buyer is willing to pay
Ask Price — the lowest price a seller is willing to accept
Spread — the difference between bid and ask price; smaller spread = more active and liquid stock
Order Queue — all pending buy and sell orders waiting to be executed
Market Depth — a real-time view of all buy and sell orders at different price levels; shows market demand and supply strength
Example: If many buyers are waiting at NPR 500 for a stock, it shows strong buying pressure.
04 🏛️ Market Structure & Indices ›

Indices help you understand the overall market direction, not just individual stocks.

Market Capitalization — total value of a company in the market = Share Price × Total Shares; larger companies like banks have higher market cap
Free Float — shares available for public trading, excluding locked promoter shares
NEPSE Index — overall indicator of market performance; if it rises, most stocks are generally performing well
Sector Indices — Banking Index, Hydropower Index, Finance Index; each sector moves differently based on economic conditions
Circuit Breaker — a safety mechanism that temporarily stops trading when prices move too fast; prevents panic buying or selling
Upper & Lower Circuit — maximum allowed price increase or drop in a single day
05 🧠 Market Behavior — Why Prices Move ›

Markets are not just numbers — they are driven by human behavior.

Supply and Demand — if more people want to buy a stock than sell it, the price increases
News Impact — positive news increases demand; negative news increases selling pressure
Investor Sentiment — Fear → selling pressure; Excitement → buying pressure. Sometimes prices move based on emotions, not logic
Example: If a hydro project gets government approval, its shares may rise quickly.

LEVEL 04 · INTERMEDIATE

Fundamental Analysis

Evaluate the true financial health of a company before investing.

★ Start here ⌄
01 📊 Financial Performance — Core Profitability Metrics ›

This group tells whether a company is making money and how efficiently it is doing so. EPS, PE, ROE, and ROA together answer: "Is this company actually profitable and efficient?"

EPS (Earnings Per Share) — how much profit a company earns per share; if EPS is high, the company is generally profitable
PE Ratio (Price to Earnings) — how much investors pay for 1 rupee of earnings; High PE = high expectations (growth stock); Low PE = undervalued or slow growth
ROE (Return on Equity) — how efficiently a company uses shareholder money to generate profit; higher ROE = better efficiency
ROA (Return on Assets) — how well a company uses its assets to generate profit; useful for banks and capital-heavy companies

PE is directly connected to EPS. If EPS increases but price stays the same, PE decreases — the stock becomes relatively cheaper.

Example: If a bank earns strong profit and EPS increases, investor confidence rises. If two banks earn the same profit but one uses less capital, it has higher ROE.
02 💰 Financial Strength — Stability & Safety ›

This group shows whether the company is financially stable or risky — answering: "Is this company financially safe?"

Book Value — real accounting value of a company per share; if market price is much higher, investors are paying a premium for future growth
Debt Ratio — how much debt a company has compared to its assets; High debt = higher risk; Low debt = safer company
Net Profit Margin — how much profit remains after expenses; higher margin = better cost control
Cash Flow — actual money movement in and out of a company; a company can show profit but still fail if cash flow is weak
Example: Hydropower companies often use loans, so the debt ratio matters a lot when evaluating them.
03 📈 Growth & Future Potential ›

This group answers: "Will this company grow in the future?"

Revenue Growth — shows whether company sales are increasing year by year
Profit Growth — shows whether net profit is increasing consistently
Dividend History — shows how regularly the company shares profit with investors
04 🏢 Ownership & Structure ›

This is very important in Nepal's market.

Promoter Holding — how much ownership is held by founders; high promoter confidence usually means strong long-term belief in the company
Institutional Holding — shares owned by banks, funds, or big institutions; high institutional interest often signals strong fundamentals
Management Quality — although not numeric, good leadership strongly affects company performance
05 ✅ A Strong Company — Putting It All Together ›

A strong company usually has all of the following. Fundamental analysis is about: "Is this company worth owning for the long term?"

Good EPS
Reasonable PE
High ROE
Low debt
Strong cash flow
Stable revenue growth
Healthy promoter holding

LEVEL 05 · INTERMEDIATE

Technical Analysis

Use price charts and indicators to decide when to buy or sell.

★ Start here ⌄
01 📊 Price Structure — Candlestick & Timeframes ›

This forms the base of all chart analysis.

Candlestick — shows price movement of a stock in a specific time period; it shows Open price, Close price, High, and Low
Timeframes — charts can be viewed in 1 day, 1 week, 1 month; short timeframe = trading; long timeframe = investing
Trend — Uptrend (price rising), Downtrend (price falling), Sideways (no clear movement)
Example: If NIC Asia Bank opens low but closes high, it shows buying strength.
02 📉 Support & Resistance ›
Support — a price level where buying is strong and price stops falling
Resistance — a price level where selling is strong and price stops rising
Breakout — when price crosses resistance or support, it often moves strongly in that direction
Example: If a stock breaks resistance at NPR 500, it may move higher quickly.
03 📊 Indicators — Mathematical Tools ›

These are tools used to analyze momentum and strength.

Moving Average (MA) — shows average price over time; helps identify trend direction and smooths price fluctuations
RSI (Relative Strength Index) — shows whether a stock is overbought or oversold; above 70 = overbought; below 30 = oversold
MACD — shows momentum and trend change signals; used to identify buying or selling opportunities
Bollinger Bands — shows volatility and price range; price touching upper band = possible overbought condition; lower band = possible oversold condition
04 📊 Volume Analysis — Market Strength ›
Volume — number of shares traded; High volume + rising price = strong trend; High volume + falling price = strong selling
VWAP (Volume Weighted Average Price) — shows the fair average price based on trading activity; helps identify whether price is above or below fair value
05 🧩 Chart Patterns — Market Behavior ›
Double Top / Bottom — shows possible reversal of trend
Head and Shoulders — indicates trend reversal from bullish to bearish
Triangle Pattern — shows consolidation before breakout

Technical analysis answers: "When should I enter or exit a stock?" It is based on price, volume, patterns, and market psychology.
